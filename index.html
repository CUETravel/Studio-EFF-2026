Reminder: Almost all Fringe performances operate on General Admission seating. Your ticket guarantees you entry to the room, but not a specific seat. Fringe venues enforce a zero-tolerance lock-out policy. If you are even 30 seconds late for a performance, the doors are permanently barred, your ticket is canceled, and you will not get a refund. Walking Tour of Dean Village
Dean Village
Few parts of Edinburgh are as picturesque as Dean Village. Once beyond the city's edge, "the Dean" is now a tucked-away gem, with 17th-century architecture, green space and the Water of Leith running through it. As you wander, watch for herons, woodpeckers, kingfishers and even otters — in the city centre! You may even spot a waterfall or two. Follow the river into Stockbridge, officially Scotland's least deprived district and a lovely place to linger, with independent shops, artisan bakeries, restaurants and pubs. From there, it's on to the New Town — but don't let the name fool you. Dating back to the 1760s, it has a rich story shaped by inventors, scientists and big thinkers.

Founded in 1981 and first shown in Edinburgh in 1983, Dazzle has become a much-anticipated highlight of the festival calendar, celebrated for championing independent design and pushing the boundaries of wearable art. Museums & Galleries

Edinburgh is wonderfully rich in museums — and most of the major ones are free.

For Art Lovers — The National Galleries of Scotland
1. The Scottish National Gallery
Open daily 10am–5pm · Free admission
The Mound, Edinburgh EH2 2EL · 9-minute walk from The Scotsman Hotel
The main building, a neoclassical William Playfair design located on the Mound (between Princes Street and the Old Town), holds some of the city's most breathtaking art collections, with works by Vermeer, Titian, Rembrandt, Velázquez, and the Impressionists, plus the newly opened Scottish Galleries featuring William McTaggart, Charles Rennie Mackintosh, and the Glasgow Boys.
  • The Bridgewater Madonna (Raphael) — an exquisite, intimate depiction of the Virgin and Child, one of the finest High Renaissance paintings in the world.
  • The Virgin Adoring the Sleeping Christ Child (Botticelli) — a beautiful, serene Renaissance piece.
  • An Old Woman Cooking Eggs (Velázquez, 1618) — a stunning masterclass in realism, light, and texture.
  • Haystacks: Snow Effect (Monet, 1891) and Olive Trees (Van Gogh, 1889), plus works by Degas, Gauguin, and Cézanne.
  • The Reverend Robert Walker Skating on Duddingston Loch (Raeburn, c. 1795) — arguably Scotland's most famous and beloved painting.
  • The Monarch of the Glen (Landseer, 1851) and The Reconciliation of Oberon and Titania (Paton, 1847).
Special exhibitions: Catherine Opie: To Be Seen (opening August 8, 2026) — the first major UK museum exhibition and first Scottish solo show for the acclaimed American photographer; the gallery extends its daily closing time to 6:00 PM in August to accommodate festival crowds. Also on display through early autumn: JMW Turner's Norham Castle, Sunrise, on special loan from the Tate.
nationalgalleries.org/visit/scottish-national-gallery
2. The Scottish National Portrait Gallery
Open daily 10am–5pm · Free admission
1 Queen St, Edinburgh EH2 1JD · 11-minute walk from The Scotsman Hotel
A red sandstone Gothic palace on Queen Street — the world's first purpose-built portrait gallery, as famous for its stunning architecture as for its art. The breathtaking entry hall features a massive gold-leaf mural telling Scotland's story through the faces of its key figures.
  • The Great Hall — the Processional Frieze mural and the building itself, modeled after the Doge's Palace in Venice with stained glass and a star-studded ceiling.
  • Modern Legends (First Floor): Tilda Swinton, Sir Sean Connery, and Alan Cumming, among Scotland's trailblazing modern figures.
  • Royal Icons & Historic Figures (Top Floor): Mary Queen of Scots, Bonnie Prince Charlie, and Robert Burns.
nationalgalleries.org/visit/scottish-national-portrait-gallery
3+4. Scottish National Gallery of Modern Art (Modern One & Modern Two)
Open daily 10am–5pm · Free admission
73/75 Belford Rd, Edinburgh EH4 3DR · 16-minute taxi or 40-minute walk
One site, two sculpture parks, two galleries. Housed in a neoclassical 1825 building near Dean Village, showcasing world-class contemporary art from 1900 onward — Picasso, Nicodemus, Redpath, and more, across the past 120 years.
Modern One holds one of the world's most comprehensive Surrealist collections — Dalí, Magritte, Ernst, and Giacometti. Peter Doig's Morning, Paramin is on display until January 2027, one of the artist's most celebrated late works, shown in Scotland for the first time.
Modern Two, twinned with Modern One across Belford Road, holds a recreation of Eduardo Paolozzi's studio and Richard Wright's The Stairwell Project, the 2009 Turner Prize winner's site-specific installation.
nationalgalleries.org/visit/scottish-national-gallery-modern-art
City Art Centre
Daily 10am (last entry 4:30pm) · Generally free
2 Market St, Edinburgh EH1 1DE · 3-minute walk from The Scotsman Hotel
Directly opposite Waverley Station, devoted to Scottish visual and applied arts — the ground floor houses a gift shop and the café, Mimi's Bakehouse. "Over recent years, Edinburgh's City Art Centre has proved to be the most important place for seeing Scottish art." — The Scotsman
Opportunities Other Than Art
National Museum of Scotland
10am–5pm daily · Free
Chambers St, Edinburgh EH1 1JF · 8-minute walk from The Scotsman Hotel
The most diverse option, with collections ranging from ancient Egypt to Dolly the sheep. Formed from a merger of the Museum of Scotland and the Royal Scottish Museum — could easily fill half a day.
nms.ac.uk/national-museum-of-scotland
The People's Story Museum
Open Mon & Thu–Sat 10–5 (closed Tue/Wed)
163 Canongate, Royal Mile, Edinburgh EH8 8BN · 8-minute walk
Explores the lives of Edinburgh's working-class generations, including artefacts from social and political movements like Women's Suffrage.
The Writers' Museum
Open Wed–Sun 10–5 (closed Mon/Tue)
Lawnmarket, Lady Stair's Close, Edinburgh EH1 2PA · 8-minute walk
Celebrates Robert Burns, Sir Walter Scott, and Robert Louis Stevenson through real artefacts — books, portraits, rare objects, and a printing press used for some of Scott's novels. A natural fit for our theatre-minded group.
The Wonderfully Unusual
Surgeons' Hall Museum
Open daily 10–5
Nicolson St, Edinburgh EH8 9DW · 8-minute walk from The Scotsman Hotel
Owned by the Royal College of Surgeons of Edinburgh, home to the largest and most historic collection of pathological objects. Dating to 1832, it covers the evolution of surgical instruments, the first public dissection, and figures like Burke, Hare, Lister, and Simpson. Genuinely fascinating, and not for the faint-hearted.
St Cecilia's Hall Concert Room and Music Museum
Open Tue–Sat 10–5 (closed Sun/Mon) · Free admission
50 Niddry St, Edinburgh EH1 1LG · 4-minute walk from The Scotsman Hotel
Part of the University of Edinburgh, an intimate Georgian concert hall housing one of the world's most important collections of musical instruments — harpsichords, clavichords, and early keyboards — with occasional concerts using the historic instruments.
